LPG at Viada costs €0.985 per litre right now. Petrol 95 at Circle K is €1.844. That gap - nearly 47% cheaper per litre - is hard to ignore. But the saving at the pump and the saving in your wallet are not the same number, and the difference matters when you're deciding whether to convert your car or buy a factory CNG vehicle. Here is what the numbers actually show.

## What Is LPG and What Is CNG?

LPG (liquefied petroleum gas) is a byproduct of natural gas processing and oil refining, stored as a liquid under moderate pressure. Most LPG kits are aftermarket additions bolted onto a petrol engine - the car runs on petrol from start, then switches to gas once the engine is warm. The system is mature technology, widely serviced across Latvia, and a converted car still passes a standard Latvian roadworthiness test provided the tank has a valid inspection stamp.

CNG (compressed natural gas) is methane stored at very high pressure - around 200 bar - in dedicated reinforced cylinders. CNG cars are almost always factory-fitted, not converted. The main names in the Latvian used-car market are VW Golf/Passat TGI, Skoda Octavia G-Tec, SEAT Leon TGI, and Fiat Panda Natural Power. Unlike LPG, you cannot simply retrofit a CNG system to an ordinary petrol car at a workshop.

## The Real Saving Per Kilometre

The per-litre price gap with LPG is dramatic, but LPG contains roughly 20% less energy per litre than petrol. A car that drinks 7 litres of petrol per 100 km will typically use around 8.5-9 litres of LPG over the same distance. That erodes the headline saving considerably.

Here is how the maths works out with current prices:

| Fuel | Price | Consumption (100 km) | Cost per 100 km |
|---|---|---|---|
| Petrol 95 (Circle K) | €1.844/L | 7.0 L | €12.91 |
| LPG (Viada) | €0.985/L | 8.8 L | €8.67 |
| LPG (Virši / Circle K) | €1.085/L | 8.8 L | €9.55 |

The cheapest LPG option saves roughly €4.24 per 100 km compared to petrol 95 at Circle K. That is a genuine 33% reduction in fuel cost per kilometre, not 47%. Still meaningful - but not the headline figure.

CNG is priced differently because it is sold by the kilogram, not the litre. Virši currently charges €1.774/kg. One kilogram of CNG holds about 25% more energy than one litre of LPG, so the comparison requires care. A CNG car doing 7 kg per 100 km (a reasonable estimate for a VW Golf TGI) pays around €12.42 per 100 km at current prices - roughly comparable to petrol, or slightly cheaper. CNG's advantage is environmental (lower CO2 per km) more than it is financial at today's prices.

## Which Stations Carry What

LPG is available at three chains in the tracked network: Virši, Circle K, and Viada. Viada is currently the cheapest at €0.985/L, with Virši and Circle K both at €1.085/L.

CNG is available only at Virši among the four chains tracked on The Fuel Pulse. Nationally, the CNG network in Latvia is thin - a handful of stations concentrated around Riga. If you commute within Riga or along the A2/A3 corridors, you can likely make it work. For drivers based in smaller towns or doing long rural routes, the range anxiety is real: a typical factory CNG tank gives 300-400 km before you need gas, and finding a CNG station outside Riga requires planning. Cold Latvian winters also reduce CNG range slightly, as the gas does not vaporise as efficiently from the high-pressure cylinder when temperatures drop below -10°C.

## LPG Conversion: Cost and Break-Even

A proper LPG installation in Latvia - sequential injection system, reputable brand like BRC or Lovato, fitted at a certified workshop - costs roughly €800 to €1,200 depending on engine size and tank capacity. Budget kits exist for less, but the certified route matters for warranty, resale, and the mandatory periodic tank inspection required under Latvian regulations (every 10 years for steel tanks, every 5 for composite).

Break-even calculation for a typical commuter:

- Annual distance: 15,000 km
- Petrol consumption: 7 L/100 km, totalling 1,050 L/year
- Annual petrol cost at €1.844: **€1,936**
- LPG consumption: 8.8 L/100 km, totalling 1,320 L/year
- Annual LPG cost at €0.985: **€1,300**
- Annual saving: **€636**
- Conversion kit at €1,000 - break-even in roughly **19 months**

At 20,000 km/year the break-even drops to around 14 months. At 10,000 km/year it stretches to nearly three years. Plug your own numbers in: multiply your annual kilometres by 0.088 to get approximate LPG litres needed, multiply by €0.985 or €1.085 depending on where you fill up, and compare to your current petrol spend.

## Practical Considerations Before Converting

The LPG tank goes in the boot. A typical 50-litre toroidal (doughnut-shaped) tank fits in the spare-wheel well and takes up little usable space, but a cylindrical tank can eat 150-200 litres of boot volume. If you carry tools, children's buggies, or ski equipment regularly, this matters.

Some underground and enclosed car parks in Latvia prohibit LPG vehicles - the rule is not universally enforced but it is legally in force in many facilities. If you park in a multi-storey or basement garage daily, check the rules before converting.

CNG cars carry their cylinders in the roof lining or under the floor, so boot space is unaffected - a meaningful practical advantage.

## Who Should Actually Consider This

LPG conversion makes clear financial sense for high-mileage drivers: daily commuters covering 25,000 km or more per year, tradespeople with loaded vans, courier drivers. The break-even arrives quickly and the ongoing savings compound over the car's life.

It makes less sense for low-mileage urban drivers doing under 10,000 km/year. The break-even extends past three years, the car depreciates, and you carry the compliance cost of tank inspections and certification paperwork for marginal gain.

CNG factory cars suit drivers already in the market for a new or nearly-new vehicle who want lower running costs and can live within the Riga-centric refuelling network. For the right driver, a VW Passat TGI bought at a reasonable used-car price is a genuinely economical choice. For everyone else, it requires a careful look at where you drive.
